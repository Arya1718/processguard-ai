# SOP-COOL-014: Cooling Water Flow and Vibration Response

## Purpose

Detect and respond to cooling-water flow loss combined with abnormal pump
vibration before heat rejection is lost. This procedure applies to cooling
water pumps (e.g. CP-series) on Site 12 and similar paper-mill installations.

## Normal operating range

Cooling water flow 118-132 m3/h, pump vibration below 2.5 mm/s RMS, supply
temperature 29-32 degC. Conductivity 950-1100 uS/cm indicates healthy
makeup-water chemistry; sustained rise suggests recirculation loss.

## Symptoms of a blocked suction or degrading pump

- Flow rate drops more than 15% below the normal band while the pump is
  energized.
- Vibration rises above the 2.5 mm/s threshold, often together with the flow
  drop.
- Supply water temperature climbs as heat rejection falls; pH may drift down
  as recirculation stagnates.

## Immediate response procedure

1. Inspect the pump suction strainer for blockage; a clogged strainer is the
   most common cause of a simultaneous flow drop and vibration rise.
2. Check filter differential pressure; replace or backwash the filter if the
   differential exceeds the posted limit.
3. Verify pump condition: bearing temperature, seal leakage, and impeller
   wear. A degrading pump shows the same combined signature (falling flow,
   rising vibration) and must be scheduled for maintenance.
4. Reduce process load if supply temperature continues rising after the
   steps above; protect the heat exchangers first.
5. If flow cannot be restored within 30 minutes, escalate to the shift
   engineer and switch to the standby pump.

## Escalation

Escalate immediately if two or more of flow, vibration, and temperature are
outside their normal bands at the same time -- this combined pattern usually
means mechanical degradation rather than a sensor fault.
